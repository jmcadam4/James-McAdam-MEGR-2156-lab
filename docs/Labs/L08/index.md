# L08 - Make Something Useful

## Design Thinking Reading

The reading had a lot of useful information however the part that stuck out to me was the "Who, What, Where, When, Why, and How" section. I design a lot of things that solve small problems I have but are not commercially viable however these questions will become a lot more relevant as I move into industry. The answers are obvious when I am thinking of them in the context of an issue right in front of me but they become far more complex when you broaden the scope to situations that effect thousands in different ways.

## Problem Statement

The problem I have been having is a loose cable in the hallway to my bedroom, I want it to be tidy to keep a tripping hazard to a minimum and ensure the cord doesn't get ripped out of the wall and thus ruin my print. I am designing this product for myself however it would work for any .325 inch cable. I noticed the problem when I was extensively cleaning my room and that was the last thing left that was bothering me. It still won't be perfect as I can't conceal it and it also plugs in and goes out perpendicular from the wall for a bit. They make adaptors for the socket to get it to be 90 degrees pointed downwards but I would prefer not to spend money on that. It would also be another point of failure that could mess up my prints and I can't risk that either.

## Design

The part I decided to make for this project is a simple cable organizer. Currently my 3d printer is in the closet however I do not have an outlet in there so I have had a loose cable running across my floor for the past few weeks. If I could just get it more tucked away against the wall I would be happy. In order to do this, I measured my cable to be 0.325 inches in diameter, then designed for a total diameter of .35 with tolerance included. Once this was done all that I needed was a flat surface to mount it to the wall with and a notch to make sure it snaps into place. I know the cable will conform as I push it into the part as the insulation is very thick so I was not as worried about designing the flanges to deform the right amount. I would rather the flanges be thicker and hold up over time so this works out well for the item. I put notches to hold the cable in touching the .35 diameter construction circle so it should still have a secure hold with the real cable. The part was super simple as it is not a hard problem to solve but not one I want to throw more than a few cents of filament at.

## Print

I printed this part with low infill but 4 walls. I want it to be strong but use minimal filament as it is not going to see very diverse stresses. I set the layer height to .25 mm, up from the .2 that is standard as there is no detail needed along layer lines as a quicker print time would be preferable. I duplicated it to get four parts and sliced. It is a super simple part that doesn't need any crazy settings but with these the print time is just a touch over 30 minutes.

## Lessons learned

Fail fast is the biggest thing, I "wasted" a lot of time working through a very complex design for a wrist mechanism that did not agree with my finances or timeline and was thus left with very little when it came to the last few days to do this lab. I still thought of something that would solve a real issue but it is not nearly as much of a portfolio piece as I would have wanted. I still intent to complete the wrist mechanism but my time should have been allocated better.
